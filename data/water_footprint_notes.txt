WATER FOOTPRINT BASICS

A water footprint measures the freshwater used or consumed in producing goods and services.

Water-footprint values are estimates and can vary depending on geographic location, climate, production methods, farming practices, and the methodology used.

Product water-footprint values should therefore not be treated as universally fixed numbers.

RICE

Rice can have a significant water footprint because water is required during cultivation.

Paddy rice may require water for crop water needs, soil saturation, percolation and seepage, and maintaining a water layer.

Irrigation requirements vary depending on climate, rainfall, soil conditions, crop characteristics, and farming practices.

Potential approaches for improving water efficiency in rice cultivation include improving irrigation management, reducing unnecessary water use, and adopting farming practices suited to local soil, climate, and water conditions.
A 2025 study indexed by FAO AGRIS estimated that producing 1 kg of paddy in India takes approximately 1500–2000 litres of water on average. This is an estimate and can vary significantly by region and production conditions.

COFFEE

Coffee production has a water footprint because water is required during the production of coffee.

The water footprint of coffee can vary depending on where and how the coffee is produced.

Water-footprint estimates should therefore be treated as estimates rather than universal fixed values.

COTTON

Cotton has a water footprint because water can be required during cotton cultivation and production.

The water footprint of cotton can vary depending on geographic location, climate, agricultural practices, irrigation methods, and production conditions.

Improving water efficiency in cotton production can involve better water management and farming practices appropriate to local conditions.

MILK

Milk has a water footprint because water is associated with producing the resources required for dairy production.

The water footprint of milk can vary depending on factors such as animal feed, production systems, geographic location, climate, and farming practices.

Water-footprint values should be interpreted as estimates and not as universal fixed values.

CHOCOLATE

Chocolate has a water footprint because water is associated with producing its agricultural ingredients and processing them into chocolate.

The water footprint can vary depending on the source and production conditions of the ingredients.

Consumers should interpret water-footprint estimates as indicators rather than universally applicable fixed values.

RESPONSIBLE AI

AquaLens AI should clearly communicate that water-footprint values are estimates.

The system should avoid presenting uncertain information as fact.

When reliable information is unavailable, AquaLens AI should say that the available knowledge base does not contain enough information.

AquaLens AI should not invent water-footprint values, recommendations, or sources.
